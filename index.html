Part One — The World in Your Cup

Before it reaches your cup,
it crosses the world

Coffee is one of the most traded agricultural commodities on earth — and Australia sits at the end of a supply chain spanning more than 50 countries. Before a bean reaches a Melbourne laneway café, it has already crossed oceans, seasons, and supply shocks.

Where Australia's Coffee Comes From

A supply chain that spans 50+ countries

A dense band across Latin America — Brazil, Colombia, Honduras — supplies over 60% of all imports. East Africa and South-East Asia fill the rest. Melbourne's café identity rests on a supply chain that could be disrupted by a drought on the other side of the world.

Bean Species

Not all beans are equal

Two species dominate global supply: Arabica — mild, aromatic, favoured by specialty cafés — and Robusta — cheaper, bolder, dominant in instant coffee and espresso blends. The preference gap between them tells you everything about how Australia takes its coffee.

~70% global consumption is Arabica
~30% Robusta — blends & instant

Top 10 Exporters, 2025

Brazil leads, but the chain is long

Brazil leads with 37,000 thousand 60 kg bags — roughly 31% of global supply. Vietnam follows with 24,600 thousand bags of mostly Robusta. Together the top three countries account for nearly 62% of all global exports.

Indonesia rises notably to fourth. African exporters Ethiopia and Uganda round out the top six, bringing high-quality Arabica varieties increasingly found on Australian café menus.

"A crop failure in Minas Gerais, Brazil can send coffee prices across the world surging within a season."

Global Consumption 2023

How much coffee does the world drink?

The choropleth maps total consumption in 2023 — darker means more. The United States leads at 1.4 million tonnes, followed by Brazil and Germany. Australia sits at roughly 47,000 tonnes — about 1.8 kg per person — a figure that understates the cultural intensity of its café scene.

Part Two — The Culture

A country unlike most others

The beans land in Australia and meet a culture that has turned coffee into a defining feature of daily life — a ritual built suburb by suburb, state by state. Nowhere else in the world orders quite like this.

What Australia Orders

Milk-based espresso, by a long shot

Walk into any café and the story is clear: milk-based espresso drinks dominate. The latte leads at around 23%, with Americano with Milk and Cappuccino close behind. The flat white — an Australian invention that conquered London and New York — is not far off.

The "Others" category hints at growing curiosity for specialty drinks. Espresso and Cortado remain niche despite strong cultural roots in Italian-Australian communities.

Australia's Café Footprint

NSW and Victoria hold over 60% of all venues

Each dot represents a cluster of cafés and restaurants, sized by the number of venues per state. The density in coastal urban centres tells the story of where coffee culture is most embedded — while the NT's sparse dot reflects both geography and population.

Google Trends, Australia 2004–2026

How Australia searches for coffee

Steady baseline interest, seasonal peaks, a surge in specialty coffee interest from 2015, and a notable dip during COVID lockdowns of 2020 — when café culture moved briefly indoors and online.

Part Three — The Price of the Ritual

Follow the bean all the way to the cup

Three things become clear at the end of the supply chain: who drinks it, what it costs, and why the price keeps climbing. The answers are shaped by geography, economics, and decades of volatile commodity markets.

Caffeine Intake by Age Group

Young adults lead, but every generation drinks

Bubble size encodes each age group's share of total caffeine consumption from sweetened beverages. Young adults aged 18–29 lead at 21 mg/day. Older Australians draw their caffeine from traditional brewed coffee rather than sweetened drinks.

Green Coffee Bean Price, AUD/lb 1969–2024

The rising cost of green coffee

The annual average price of green coffee beans — converted to AUD — tells a volatile story. Three events stand out: the 1977 Brazilian frost sent prices to overnight record highs; the 1997 Asian demand boom drove another surge; and the 2011 global commodity spike pushed prices to levels not seen since the 1970s.

But nothing compares to today. A devastating drought and frost season in Brazil pushed AUD prices to record highs in 2024–25. The dashed trend line is unmistakably upward — a crop that cost under $1 AUD per pound in the early 2000s now exceeds $5. Drag the navigator to zoom into any period.

Flat White Price by State — Deviation from $4.99 National Average

What a flat white costs depends on where you live

The flat white is Australia's benchmark coffee — and its price varies more than you might expect. Darwin leads at $5.34, almost $0.45 above the cheapest state. New South Wales, despite hosting the most cafés in the country, comes in at just $4.89.

Victoria sits at exactly the national average of $4.99 — fitting for the state that defines Australia's coffee standard. The NT premium reflects the economics of remote business: higher freight, fewer competitors, and a smaller customer base spreading fixed costs across fewer cups each day.

City-Level Prices, Grouped by State

Zoom in and an interesting pattern emerges

Within each state, city prices are remarkably consistent. Brisbane, Gold Coast, Sunshine Coast and Cairns all sit at exactly $5.03. Adelaide, Barossa Valley and Victor Harbor all share $5.13. This consistency tells us that pricing is driven by state-level factors — wages, rent, supply logistics — rather than individual city dynamics.

The one clear outlier is Darwin and Alice Springs in the NT, which stand alone at $5.35, well above every other city in the country. Hover over any dot to see the city name and price.
