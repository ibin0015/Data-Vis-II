Part One

The World in Your Cup

Before a bean reaches an Australian café, it crosses oceans. Coffee is one of the most traded agricultural commodities on earth — and Australia sits at the end of a supply chain spanning more than 50 countries.

Where Australia's Coffee Comes From

The map traces green coffee bean imports into Australia. A dense band across Latin America — Brazil, Colombia, Honduras — supplies over 60% of all imports. East Africa and South-East Asia fill the rest. Melbourne's café identity rests on a supply chain that could be disrupted by a drought on the other side of the world.

Not All Beans Are Equal

Two species dominate global supply: Arabica — mild, aromatic, favoured by specialty cafés — and Robusta — cheaper, bolder, dominant in instant coffee and espresso blends. The preference gap between them tells you everything about how Australia takes its coffee.

~70% global consumption is Arabica
~30% Robusta — blends & instant

Top 10 Coffee Bean Exporters, 2025

Brazil leads with 37,000 thousand 60 kg bags — roughly 31% of global supply. Vietnam follows with 24,600 thousand bags of mostly Robusta. Together the top three countries account for nearly 62% of all global exports.

Indonesia rises notably to fourth. African exporters Ethiopia and Uganda round out the top six, bringing high-quality Arabica varieties increasingly found on Australian café menus.

"A crop failure in Minas Gerais, Brazil can send coffee prices across the world surging within a season."

Coffee Consumption by Country

The choropleth maps total consumption in 2023 — darker means more. The United States leads at 1.4 million tonnes, followed by Brazil and Germany. Australia sits at roughly 47,000 tonnes — about 1.8 kg per person.

Part Two

The Culture

The beans land in Australia and meet a country unlike most others. Nowhere here has turned coffee into quite such a defining feature of daily life — a ritual built suburb by suburb, state by state.

What Australia Orders

Walk into any café and the story is clear: milk-based espresso drinks dominate. The latte leads at around 23%, with Americano with Milk and Cappuccino close behind. The flat white — an Australian invention that conquered London and New York — is not far off.

The "Others" category hints at growing curiosity for specialty drinks. Espresso and Cortado remain niche despite strong cultural roots in Italian-Australian communities.

Australia's Café Footprint

Each dot represents a cluster of cafés and restaurants, sized by the number of venues per state. NSW and Victoria dominate, together accounting for over 60% of all venues. The NT's sparse dot reflects both geography and population.

How Australia Searches for Coffee

Google Trends data from 2004 to 2026 tracks search interest in "coffee" and "café" across Australia. Steady baseline interest, seasonal peaks, a surge in specialty coffee interest from 2015, and a notable dip during COVID lockdowns of 2020.

Part Three

The Price of the Ritual

Follow the bean all the way to the cup in your hand at 8am — and three things become clear: who drinks it, what it costs, and why the price keeps climbing.

Caffeine Intake Across Generations

Bubble size encodes each age group's share of total caffeine consumption from sweetened beverages. Young adults aged 18–29 lead at 21 mg/day. Older Australians draw their caffeine from traditional brewed coffee rather than sweetened drinks.

The Rising Cost of Green Coffee

The annual average price of green coffee beans — converted to AUD — tells a volatile story. The 1977 Brazilian frost, the 1997 Asian demand boom, and the 2011 global commodity spike each drove sharp price surges.

The dashed trend line is unmistakably upward. A crop that cost under $1 AUD per pound in the early 2000s now exceeds $5. Drag the bottom navigator to zoom into any time period.

What a Flat White Costs by State

Darwin leads at $5.35, almost $0.45 above the cheapest. New South Wales, despite hosting the most cafés in the country, comes in at $4.89. Victoria sits at exactly the national average of $4.99 — fitting for the state that defines Australia's coffee standard.

The NT premium reflects the cost of remote business: higher freight, fewer competitors, and a smaller customer base spreading fixed costs across fewer cups.

Flat White Price, City by City

Within states, prices are often consistent — suggesting state-level cost structures dominate local pricing. Darwin stands alone as the nation's priciest market; Sydney and the NSW cluster anchor the affordable end.
